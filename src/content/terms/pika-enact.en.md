explain: "Pika presents Enact as a step toward AI filmmaking that looks more like directing on a set than typing into a prompt box. Technically it streams low-latency generation on top of H3 Flex, taking mouse trajectories, spoken notes, and camera moves as continuous input rather than one prompt and one finished clip. The blog says that in its low-latency setting, the picture can respond in under 0.4 seconds on an H100, decoding included, and that generation can run about 1.8× faster than playback on a single server so a drag does not have to stall the video.\n\nLive Staging is described as needing tightly aligned before-and-after video pairs with frame-level cursor trajectories, which real footage rarely provides. The team built a physics-driven synthetic data engine: a 3D stand-in is moved along a drag path on an otherwise untouched clip, photorealistic targets are rendered, and mouse positions are logged automatically. That yields training pairs in which only the dragged subject changes, covering lifts, carries, and inserting a new subject from a photo into a live scene.\n\nActor direction and camera control are the other two inputs in the same real-time loop, including giving notes and turning rehearsal into a continuous take. Access, waitlists, and whether this lands in the product should be checked on the official blog and Pika’s current statements. Enact is not just another text-to-video model name; it is a live directing environment stacked on H3 Flex."
