intro: "**ChefNMR2** is a **generative model for small-molecule structure elucidation** built by **Princeton University** (the Ellen D. Zhong lab in Computer Science and the Mohammad R. Seyedsayamdost lab in Chemistry) together with AI-for-science company **Edison Scientific**. Given a molecular formula plus one-dimensional **¹H and ¹³C NMR peak lists**, it generates candidate molecular structures directly.\n\nThe paper, *Small molecule structure elucidation from NMR spectra with generative and reasoning models*, appeared on bioRxiv on October 29, 2026. On **November 2, 2026 (US time)** Edison announced that ChefNMR2 is **now available inside Kosmos**, its autonomous AI-scientist platform, so researchers can call it without running the model themselves."
explain: "## How it works\n\n- **Atomic flow matching**: structure elucidation is framed as generating atomic coordinates conditioned on the formula and NMR peaks. The architecture is a **peak encoder** plus a **multimodal diffusion transformer** with joint attention between peak tokens and atom tokens. At inference, noise is transformed into coordinates, then bonds are assigned to obtain candidate structures.\n- **Calibrated confidence**: repeated sampling yields an empirical confidence (how often each planar structure appears). After an initial 100 samples, the sampling budget adapts to that confidence.\n- **ChefNMR2-Agent**: an LLM reasoning agent (running on Kosmos) wraps the generator, adding a natural-language interface, chemical reasoning, computational tools and literature search, and digging deeper only when the model is uncertain.\n\n## Key numbers\n\n| Metric | Value |\n|---|---|\n| Training data | 2.52M molecules with experimental and simulated spectra |\n| Evaluation | 2,031 molecules across five test sets |\n| ChefNMR2 top-1 | **82.3%** |\n| Best specialized de novo model (NMRTransformer) | 35.4% |\n| Retrieval method (NMR-Solver) | 60.1% |\n| Best LLM (Claude Opus 5) | 52.0% |\n| Speed / cost | up to 2,170x lower median runtime, 360x lower median cost per molecule |\n\nThe team used ChefNMR2-Agent to solve the structures of **four novel natural products**, matching human-chemist accuracy in a fraction of the time.\n\n## Caveats\n\nThe work is a preprint and not yet peer reviewed. Some baselines had training overlap with test molecules, and accuracy falls to roughly 57% on molecules that are both large and dissimilar from the training data. The authors position it as **ranked candidates for expert review**, not an autonomous certificate of molecular identity."
usage: "## Who it is for\n\n- Researchers in **natural products, medicinal chemistry and synthesis** who need to infer unknown small-molecule structures from 1D NMR quickly.\n- Labs and CROs with large spectral archives that want batch first-pass structure proposals.\n- AI-for-science practitioners interested in the \"specialized generator + general reasoning agent\" pattern.\n\n## Getting started\n\n1. Read the project page at chefnmr.cs.princeton.edu and the bioRxiv preprint for input format (formula + ¹H/¹³C peak lists) and limitations.\n2. Use ChefNMR2 / ChefNMR2-Agent inside Edison Scientific's **Kosmos** platform: provide peak lists and sample context.\n3. Review the ranked candidates and confidence: confirm high-confidence answers quickly, and let the agent (or a chemist) investigate low-confidence ones.\n4. Validate key conclusions experimentally (2D NMR, mass spectrometry, etc.)."
