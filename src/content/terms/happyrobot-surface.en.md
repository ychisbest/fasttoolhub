intro: "**HappyRobot Surface** (official product name **Surface**) is HappyRobot’s process-discovery product, launched on 18 November 2026, for seeing how work actually gets done in an enterprise before anyone builds agents on it. The official post [Discover the Surface of work](https://www.happyrobot.ai/blog/discover-the-surface-of-work-so-you-can-reimagine-it), written by co-founder and CEO Pablo Palafox, describes Surface as a way to see the real process first, then decide what to standardize, redesign, or automate.\n\nHappyRobot says its forward-deployed engineers have spent three years sitting with operators, and that the process on paper and the process in practice often diverge. Surface turns that work into software: it records screens and activity through the browser, an agent asks about what is happening on screen in the moment, and recordings across many people are compared.\n\nThis entry records the newly launched product name Surface. It is not Microsoft’s Surface hardware, and it is not the earlier Surfaces feature on Traces. The product page is [happyrobot.ai/surface](https://www.happyrobot.ai/surface)."
explain: "The company’s claim is that many teams start automating before they can describe how the work actually runs, so agents inherit the gaps. The blog cites Deloitte research among leaders piloting agents: most expect about half of their processes to be rebuilt around agents within four years, but only about one in five say those processes are ready. Palafox argues the missing piece is often a way to see the work itself.\n\nSurface has two layers. It sees the work by recording an employee’s screens and clicks in the browser. It asks why with a HappyRobot agent that watches the live screen and questions the actual moment—why those three systems, what happens if a step is skipped—rather than following a generic script. The recording shows what someone did; the conversation captures the judgment behind it. Run across a team, Surface compares what everyone shares, where people differ, and whether someone has already found a better way.\n\nThe post also sets recording limits: employees choose when to record and can pause or stop at any time; private data is masked on the device before it is stored; recordings are encrypted in a dedicated regional store. HappyRobot says Surface studies the process, not the person, and is meant to give frontline staff better tools rather than more oversight.\n\nThe output is a map of the real process, tied back to recorded moments, plus exceptions, a time baseline, and a starting point for what to redesign, standardize, or automate first. Process owners can correct the map until the organization agrees on it. Flows chosen for automation can be turned directly into workflows on the HappyRobot platform, with no separate handoff, according to the company. Surface is in pilot with select customers today."
