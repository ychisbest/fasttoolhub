It tests whether models can connect a molecule’s structure with experimental evidence to explain results, compare compounds, and predict their properties. Official announcement: [Introducing MedChemBench](https://openai.com/index/introducing-medchembench/). The official [@OpenAI](https://x.com/OpenAI) account posted about the benchmark the same day, saying it shows how frontier models have improved at helping scientists with this work.\n\nThe official page says the benchmark has **99** problems drawn from published research, covering **117** papers and patents and **761** distinct molecular structures. At the xhigh reasoning setting, **GPT‑6.2 Astra** earned **84.5%** of the available credit, compared with **83.3%** for **GPT‑6 Astra**, **82.0%** for **GPT‑6.1 Sol**, and **58.2%** for **GPT‑5.6 Sol**. OpenAI also notes that reconstructing structures and explaining evidence were relatively stronger, while selecting compounds and predicting numerical properties proved harder."
explain: "## What problem it targets\n\nA hard part of medicinal chemistry is that improving one property of a molecule can make another worse: a compound may act strongly against a target but be poorly absorbed or break down too quickly. Chemists work through those tradeoffs by designing, making, and testing related compounds, then deciding what to try next. For AI to help, a model needs to connect structural changes with experimental evidence and reason about which compounds are worth pursuing.\n\nMedChemBench turns those judgments into scored problems. For most items, the model receives the context available at a chosen point in a research program: the biological target, the researchers’ goals, the compounds studied so far, and selected experimental findings.\n\n## What the official page says it grades\n\nAccording to OpenAI, the benchmark evaluates:\n\n- **Molecular structures**: correct atom connections and the specified stereochemistry;\n- **Scientific reasoning**: explanations supported by the supplied evidence, such as why one compound was chosen;\n- Comparing compounds and predicting numerical properties, which the official write-up says proved harder.\n\n## Limits\n\nThis is a benchmark, not a new generative model or a lab platform. The scores come from OpenAI’s official report under a stated reasoning setting and are not a discovery rate in a real laboratory. The problems are drawn from published papers and patents, so the test is reasoning from given historical evidence, not open-ended experimental work."
