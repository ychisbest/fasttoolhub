intro: "**Replit Missions** is the agent-swarm product Replit launched on 17 November 2026. The official post [Introducing Replit Missions](https://replit.com/blog/introducing-replit-missions) describes it as a way to take on complex work a team has not had time or capacity for—winning more enterprise customers, improving product performance, or replacing legacy software. You define the outcome; Replit coordinates a swarm of agents to plan, build, test, and iterate toward it within the budget and boundaries you set.\n\nDuring setup, Missions asks what success means—for example, whether a paid pilot counts as an enterprise customer, or whether success requires a signed annual contract above a minimum value. Those answers shape what the mission prioritizes and how it measures results. The agreement can also cap spending and require your approval before actions such as contacting prospects, changing customer-facing materials, or making product commitments.\n\nThis entry records the product name Replit Missions. **DataCat**, mentioned in the post, is a monitoring platform Replit built with Missions as an internal example; it is not the product name. Missions should also not be merged with an ordinary single-session Replit Agent chat."
explain: "The idea is “goal plus budget plus approvals,” not another chat box. You agree on the outcome, how much can be spent, and which steps need a person, then a multi-agent swarm explores approaches in parallel, shares findings, and checks each other’s work. Replit’s enterprise-sales example has some agents researching accounts while others build tailored demos; after approved outreach goes out, the mission reviews which messages produced qualified conversations and adjusts the next round.\n\nReplit offers two kinds of evidence. One is an internal project: bringing application monitoring in-house. A swarm built DataCat—dashboards, alerts, and tracing—in two days on a $3,000 budget, then tested the platform against measurable performance and reliability goals. The other is a NumPy benchmark across 13 representative tasks. The post says a single agent under Auto (Max) reached speedups of up to about 4×, and larger swarms went higher (public materials mention up to about 19×). Treat those figures as the blog’s published charts, not as a guarantee for every task.\n\nReplit Missions is not a new foundation model. It is a project-level orchestration product on top of Replit’s agent capabilities. When you evaluate it, separate official demo results from the cost and quality you can actually reproduce on your own work."
usage: "Read [Introducing Replit Missions](https://replit.com/blog/introducing-replit-missions) for how goals, budgets, and approvals are defined, then create a mission in the Replit product as the interface stands at the time. It is a fit for work that has a clear outcome but needs multi-step exploration: a performance campaign, a self-hosted internal tool, or a growth task that needs repeated outreach with a human still approving sensitive steps.\n\nBefore you start, write down success criteria, a spending cap, and the actions that must be approved—contacting customers, changing public copy, touching production systems. While it runs, check that agents actually stop at those boundaries, and what you still have to hand off if the budget runs out.\n\nIf you only want a single Replit agent to edit a file or write a short script, you usually do not need this new Missions product. Do not treat DataCat as a separately shipped external product name either."
