explain: "## Key points\n\n1. **Human traces**: Arbium's term for computer activity stored over time. A trace links what someone sees and does to the activity around it: the conversation that prompted an action, the document it changed and the colleague whose next decision depends on it.\n2. **Why it matters**: most workplace data records outputs. Traces show how outputs came to exist, through intermediate decisions, incomplete knowledge, corrections and coordination. Arbium's thesis is that this is the most valuable data for AI that does real work.\n3. **What each record holds**: timestamp, application and window, event type and accessibility-tree text, with screen recordings for visual context.\n4. **Benchmark**: facts planted in the story have known answers, and **430 questions** test whether a system can recover them, from simple lookups to changes over time, multi-step and team questions, and questions with no answer in the data. Frontier models scored in the high 60s to low 70s percent using only the traces, per coverage on X.\n5. **How it was generated**: Arbium specifies a fictional company, its staff, documents and story events, then has agents play the employees on virtual machines running real applications in one shared workspace. Eighteen measures of a working day's shape (burstiness, app switching, pauses and so on) are checked against ranges from real people.\n6. **Privacy**: real-work observations only inform behavioral measurements such as timing and app transitions; all business content is fictional."
