intro: "**Taan V1** is the generally available instruction-following music model from **Taan**, launched on 24 November 2026. Co-founder [@mp3addi](https://x.com/mp3addi/status/2125277353609932891) announced it at 17:16 UTC that day (01:16 on 25 November, Beijing time): “Today we're launching Taan V1,” with “significant improvements in quality and genre support over V1-preview.” Product site: [taan.ai](https://taan.ai/).\n\nTaan positions itself as “the most precise foundation music models,” focused on timeline control—what plays, when it plays, and how it changes—rather than generating a full track in one black-box pass. The company says training data is **100% licensed**, and that it uses on the order of **1/1000** the data and compute of frontier music models. This page files the GA name Taan V1; the preview is not a separate term.\n"
explain: "## What the company claims\n\nThe homepage argument is that control is a modeling problem, not a scale problem. Frontier music models, Taan says, typically go from a prompt to a full track in one pass: good for catchy songs, hard to direct. Ask for a beat drop at exactly 0:12 and most of the time you will not get it. Bigger models trained on more songs will not fix that, the company argues, because songs are not written to a single timeline and real music is not made in one pass.\n\nTaan describes a generation process closer to director, composer, then performer: first a plan, then a score, then a performance, with direction at each step about what plays, when, and how it changes. Capabilities emphasized on the site include:\n\n- **Granular edits**: change only a section, a set of instruments, or a time span, leaving bass and drums (for example) untouched, without regenerating the whole track.\n- **Multimodal input**: combine video, a script, and reference audio; starting points can also include a hum, exact notes, MIDI, or an image.\n- **Second-level timing**: a beat drop at exactly twelve seconds, or a melody change that leaves the bass and drums running.\n\n## TimingBench scores (published benchmark)\n\nIn August 2026 Taan published **TimingBench**, a temporal-controllability benchmark (*Are you rushing or dragging?*, by Yash Bhake, Adithya R, and Devansh Gupta). The snapshot on the site (250 prompts, 30-second instrumental tracks) reports:\n\n| System | Instruction success | Events within ±500 ms | Tempo | FAD |\n| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |\n| taan.ai | **96.1%** | **91.3%** | 92.0% | 4.8 |\n| ElevenLabs v2 | 28.9% | 12.8% | 87.0% | 3.5 |\n| Google Lyria | 23.9% | 8.6% | 89.7% | 2.6 |\n| ElevenLabs v1 | 25.4% | 8.0% | 93.0% | 3.5 |\n| Suno | 22.0% | 6.8% | 86.7% | 3.1 |\n\nThe company says this comparison measures control, not overall audio quality; Taan’s FAD is not better than every baseline. The 95% confidence interval on instruction success is given as 93.3–99.2. Research notes are at [taan.ai/docs](https://taan.ai/docs/). Quality and genre gains of V1 over V1-preview are a launch-day product claim; the TimingBench numbers come from the August write-up and should not be read as a fresh V1-only leaderboard run.\n"
