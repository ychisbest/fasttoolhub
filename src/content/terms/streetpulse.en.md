intro: "**StreetPulse** is a free urban-perception website launched on 24 November 2026 by researchers at the Toulouse School of Economics (TSE), at [cities.ccl.center](https://cities.ccl.center). TSE's same-day press note says the site uses AI to analyze the quality of urban space from street photographs, covering cities on six continents, for residents, local governments, and practitioners. It builds on more than a decade of urban-perception work at the Center for Collective Learning (Place Pulse, StreetScore, and related projects), associated with César A. Hidalgo and colleagues.\n\nCo-founder [@cesifoti](https://x.com/cesifoti/status/2125248027648467170) posted the launch at 15:19 UTC on 24 November (23:19 Beijing time), describing StreetPulse as a global observatory of urban perception that scores large numbers of street-level images with AI."
explain: "## What it is\n\nAccording to the [StreetPulse data page](https://cities.ccl.center/data.html) and the TSE announcement, each street image is scored from 1 to 10 on seven dimensions: **safety, liveliness, walkability, beauty, comfort, wealth, and historical character**. The project is explicit that these are AI-generated visual impressions of streets, **not** measurements of actual safety, income, or living conditions. They can miss local context and should be read alongside other sources.\n\n**StreetPulse 1.0** publishes two downloadable datasets (scores and metadata such as coordinates, not the original photographs):\n\n- **Open access**: about **2,484,293** Mapillary and Panoramax photos across **150** cities in **67** countries, scored with **Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite**.\n- **Google Street View**: about **517,141** images identified by API URL, covering **100** cities. These scores used an earlier prompt (P0) and are for validation in the paper; the site warns not to pool them with the open-access scores.\n\nThe TSE note refers to **162** cities on six continents, which does not line up one-for-one with the per-source city counts in the 1.0 data packages—use whichever page you are citing. Methodology is described in Kim et al., arXiv (2026), linked from the data page.\n\nStreetPulse is a new public site and dataset name, not a new foundation model. Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, which scored the open-access images, is also not a new name in this scan."
