explain: "## The problem it targets\n\nSenseTime frames the release around a familiar failure mode: a poster already looks good enough to publish, until you zoom in and find a typo. Until now, that usually meant the user had to proofread, re-prompt, and edit. SenseNova U1.6 tries to fold “generate, inspect, and revise” into a single generation pass. The company notes that SenseNova U1 Pro had already shown the potential of an Agentic Generation Loop; U1.6 attempts to internalize that loop in the model itself, starting with infographic generation.\n\n## Native image self-correction\n\nWith self-correction turned on, the official flow is:\n\n- **Self-check**: review the generated image against the user's intent\n- **Locate issues**: blurry text, typo/garbled characters, English spelling mistakes, and broken numbering\n- **Closed-loop revision**: iterate using the original prompt and the existing image\n- **Some generalization**: training focused on infographics, but SenseTime says the ability also showed up in some non-infographic cases\n\nOn UltraTextBench (dense infographics), SenseTime reports that U1.6 Lite scored 86.04 on a single pass and 92.28 with self-correction enabled, a relative gain of about 7%.\n\n## Aesthetics and typography\n\nThe announcement argues that “being able to draw something” is no longer the hard part; “drawing it just right” is. SenseNova U1.6 trains aesthetics models for different content types: portraits and photorealism emphasize realism, texture, and lighting; illustration emphasizes style and creativity; infographics have to balance text, layout, and overall design. In an internal user evaluation, SenseTime says U1.6 Lite scored about 10% higher on aesthetics than U1.5 Lite.\n\nFor text rendering, the company says it strengthened perspective text and decorative lettering, and introduced an infographic aesthetics model aimed at typography and layout, so accuracy does not have to come at the expense of type expression, spatial perspective, or page design. For creative posters, it claims one-shot text-and-image layouts with accurate lettering and more designed composition, including artistic, unconventional, and handwritten type. Portrait work is described as better at “camera language” such as motion blur, film grain, and lens character; illustration work is described as stronger in brushwork, material feel, and composition.\n"
