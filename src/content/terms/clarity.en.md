The official write-up is the newsroom post [Helsing unveils Clarity, a sovereign software platform for military intelligence](https://helsing.ai/newsroom/helsing-unveils-clarity-a-sovereign-software-platform-for-military-intelligence). The product page is [Clarity](https://helsing.ai/clarity). The official account [@HelsingAI](https://x.com/HelsingAI/status/2120465601873252590) posted at 2026-11-11T10:36:10Z, calling it “an AI-native agentic intelligence platform for armed forces” that is “developed in Europe, under European control.”\n\nAccording to the company, armed forces now collect more data than ever — from satellites, sensors, drones and other sources — and can also strike in more ways than ever, from long-range missiles to autonomous systems across domains. The bottleneck, the newsroom post says, sits in between: turning data into a timely, reliable operational picture and preparing the information needed to act is still done mostly by hand. Clarity is presented as the answer. It processes everything a unit has access to, links those sources, analyses large volumes of data in seconds, detects military assets and positions, presents relevant adversary assets for human review, and prepares the information required by the most suitable weapon. The product page splits the work into synthesising intelligence, identifying threats and preparing engagements: hundreds of agents watch satellite imagery, drone video, signals, open sources, field reports and systems a force already runs, flag what is new or has moved to a master agent, and turn fragmented information into one shared operational picture. Each finding gains weight only when independent sources agree, and every conclusion is traced back to its evidence. Clarity then recommends the best-suited effector and checks that the data is precise enough for it. A long-range missile needs a precise coordinate; autonomous platforms need a signature their seeker can recognise, a search area and mission parameters. Only after the operator makes the choice does Clarity build the package.\n\nOn training and architecture, the newsroom post says the platform is built around AI agents developed specifically for military use. Its models are trained on extensive military data, combining real-world data that reflects operational conditions with synthetic data and simulated environments. A shared military ontology and evidence base let hundreds of agents work in parallel, connecting evidence, using analytical tools and carrying out complex intelligence tasks. CTO Rob Fink said Clarity combines multimodal sensor-data understanding, reasoning and planning with the systematic benefits of modern software systems: data governance and access controls, traceability and provenance, and compliant handling of classified information. Co-CEO and co-founder Gundbert Scherf said a capability this critical must be sovereign: software built in Europe, running on a force’s own infrastructure and staying under its control.\n\nThe official materials repeatedly state that humans stay in command. An operator reviews the evidence, nominates every threat and authorises every hand-off to a weapon. Clarity never relies on a single source. It stops anything progressing on weak evidence or out-of-date data, builds in legal review and the rules of engagement, and records every decision together with the person who made it. Each user sees only what their clearance allows, and each force owns the infrastructure Clarity runs on. The platform follows NATO standards, connects to effectors from any manufacturer, and ships with an open SDK.
