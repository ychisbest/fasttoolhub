The official account [@FishAudio](https://x.com/FishAudio/status/2120215430807904395) posted at 2026-11-10T18:02:05Z; co-founder and CEO Rissa Cao’s launch post is [2120214987633467626](https://x.com/rissa_cao/status/2120214987633467626).\n\nThe official blog separates being fast from having a sense of timing: an agent can start a reply quickly and still speak at the wrong moment. A pause after “What time do you open?” does not mean the same thing as a pause in the middle of a phone number. Most voice agents split speech recognition, reasoning, speech generation and turn-taking across a pipeline, then stitch those parts together with rules. Live 3, Fish Audio says, learns conversational timing inside the speech model itself, from real human conversations, so it can learn when to acknowledge, wait, respond or stop. “The fix so far has been more rules and more thresholds,” Cao said in the post. “We think a model should learn this from real conversations, the same way language models learn grammar.”\n\nThe blog gives concrete behaviors. If a caller reads a number in chunks — “415… 555… 0132” — Live 3 might say “mm-hmm” or “okay, 415” and then leave the floor open so the person can breathe and continue. Officially, it can decide to respond in as little as 186 milliseconds; short reactions such as “mm-hmm,” “okay,” and quick confirmations keep the exchange moving while a full answer is prepared. If a user says “yeah” or “mm-hmm” while the agent is explaining hours or services, it keeps going. If the user says “Wait, actually, I just need Thursday,” it stops and takes the new request. When someone revises an order — “two pizzas… no, three. Three large. And… actually, make one of them a margherita” — the company says Live 3 waits through each correction instead of jumping in with the wrong item. The post includes three excerpts from test calls: finishing an order, cutting in with a request, and acknowledging while an order is read back.\n\nOn availability, the official blog says Live 3 speaks English today, is available on the Fish Agent Platform and through the API, and can be deployed on-premises for enterprises. The product page places it in a platform for building, deploying and monitoring voice agents that sound human and get work done, and lists live monitoring, turn-by-turn transcripts, latency breakdowns, automatic summaries and success criteria.
