intro: "**RoboColosseum** is a **distributed real-robot evaluation framework** for physical intelligence in the wild, from NUS, UW, Stanford, UNC, Ai2, Oxford, **Hugging Face**, **BitRobot**, and the **Singapore Embodied AI Centre (EAIC)** and partners—paper [arXiv:2611.02497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02497) (submitted **2026-11-02**). It combines unified policy serving and standardized protocols across locally operated robots in diverse environments.\n\nThe broader network vision is **100+ robots / 140+ scenes**; the published study spans SO-101, Franka (DROID), bimanual YAM, and Unitree **G1**, evaluating five VLAs and three frontier agents. Two tracks: **Open Track** (blinded pairwise trials without task-specific adaptation) and **Fine-tune Track** (same 100 demos per task). Authors pledge **10,000 free evaluation hours** for academic labs. HF / Unitree and partners amplified on **2026-11-04**."
explain: "## Problem\n\nGeneralist robot policies must work across robots, tasks, and scenes, but physical trials are hard to reproduce and single-lab samples stay small. Simulation scales, yet sensing/dynamics gaps remain. RoboColosseum distributes real robots under shared interfaces to balance comparability with environmental diversity.\n\n## Differentiation\n\n- **Multi-embodiment**: not a single Franka/DROID arena (cf. RoboArena-class efforts).\n- **Two tracks**: Open (zero-shot in-the-wild pairwise) + Fine-tune (fixed demo budget).\n- **Scale & stats**: initial study includes 5,699 Open Track comparisons at 124 sites; Difficulty-Calibrated Rasch (DCR) rankings plus active matchmaking; 10k free academic hours.\n\n## Limits\n\n- Reported numbers cover 17 Sep–28 Oct 2026; coverage depends on checkpoint/hardware fit.\n- Fine-tune fixes data but allows each model’s official recipe—end-to-end adaptation, not identical optimizers.\n- On G1, no policy fully succeeds on the five tasks; use progress scores, not success alone."
