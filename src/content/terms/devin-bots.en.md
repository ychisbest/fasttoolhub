Devin is a single agent; a Devin Bot is a layer on top of it: the bot keeps the goal, splits the work across parallel Devins, and brings a person in only when a decision needs human judgment. Same-day coverage, including Fortune, used that distinction.\n\nThis entry records the new product name rather than re-filing Devin itself. explain: "Cognition describes a Devin Bot as a persistent agent with its own memory, identity, and permissions. It breaks down an ongoing goal, coordinates multiple Devins to plan, test, and prepare pull requests, and operates inside the repository access, budgets, and human-review rules a team sets. The public pitch stresses that a bot retains context across sessions, rather than ending when a single task is done.\n\nThe official examples span a wide range of software work, from firmware to merging large volumes of PRs. Public discussion of the launch also described hand-offs such as a QA bot finding a bug and passing it to a developer bot. The underlying claim is the same: the unit of productivity is shifting from a single agent in one session to a bot that owns an outcome and leads a team of agents.\n\nAvailability and pricing follow Cognition’s product and plan pages. Public materials place the capability on Max and Team plans (Max has been listed at $200 per month); the exact inclusion, permission model, and “what a bot may merge without a human reading it” are configured in the product. Do not confuse Devin Bots with unrelated bot or avatar experiments that appeared in the same news cycle."
