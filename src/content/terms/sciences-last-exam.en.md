Core question: **Can AI do research, end to end?**\n\nResearchers from Stanford, Oxford, Princeton, and others contribute in a personal capacity; materials note writing/review by PhD students and postdocs from about **34** institutions in five countries.\n\nHighlights:\n\n- **113** public tasks across **9** disciplines;\n- **6** capability diagnostics plus **1** end-to-end research assessment (wet-lab execution still under construction/expansion);\n- Capabilities include hypothesis discovery, protocol planning, scientific computing, scientific software operation, experiment observation, and more; closed-loop tasks require agents to keep forming hypotheses, choosing experiments, handling counterexamples, and submitting verifiable artifacts;\n- Early reports: among ~**8** frontier model setups, best composite ~**37.5/100** (Claude Opus 5 + Claude Code); **0** on the true end-to-end research track in those reports;\n- Public task packages and leaderboard evaluation planned around the **2026-11-06** launch window."
explain: "## Problem\n\nMost science benchmarks stop at QA, isolated sub-tasks, or paper reproduction with the solution given. SLE targets a full research loop—hypothesis → planning → compute/software → observation → (planned) wet-lab—with expert-authored hidden checks on whether results actually hold.\n\n## Structure\n\n- **Capability diagnostics**: pinpoint which skill fails (literature/hypothesis, protocol, code, GUI software, video observation, etc.);\n- **Closed-loop end-to-end**: whether skills chain into independently verifiable research outcomes;\n- Tasks emphasize real research questions and inspectable artifacts, not multiple-choice scores.\n\n## Limits\n\n- End-to-end and wet-lab parts are still expanding; some frontier models may be pending on the leaderboard;\n- Scores such as 37.5/100 come from public launch/reporting—use the official Leaderboard as source of truth;\n- Difficulty and verification depend heavily on domain-expert task design."
