explain: "## Why CADRE\n\nVideo world models predict the next frames from past frames and an agent's actions, and they generate inside a video autoencoder's latent space. Many borrow autoencoders from text-to-video generators, which were built for offline clip generation (no agent waiting on an action) and can't easily be retrained at a different compression. CADRE was built to study how, where and how much a world model should compress video.\n\n## Architecture\n\n- **Encoder**: frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 features (averaged across layers)\n- **Bottleneck**: one small trained strided convolution that sets the temporal/spatial compression\n- **Decoder**: a space-time transformer diffusion (flow-matching) decoder, self-distilled to **1–2 steps** and causal in time\n- ~475M trained parameters + 86M frozen DINOv3 parameters\n\n## Key findings\n\n- World models trained on CADRE latents run in real time at **20 fps on a single H100** and react to actions in **under 230 ms**, across robot and human manipulation, driving and indoor navigation\n- CADRE forms most of the latency–quality frontier\n- At strong compression a diffusion decoder is sharper than perceptual regression; manipulation (still camera) favors temporal compression while navigation (moving camera) favors spatial compression"
