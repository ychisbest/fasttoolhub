explain: "## Key points\n\n- **Natural, self-paired speakers**: instead of pairing strangers with assigned topics (as in Fisher or Switchboard), the team built a WhatsApp-like calling app that people used for their normal calls with **friends and family**. The result is more interruptions, overlaps, laughter, backchannels and short gaps between turns.\n- **Wideband audio**: 48 kHz recordings, versus roughly 8 kHz telephone-band audio in older corpora like Fisher.\n- **Separate tracks**: each speaker is captured on their own channel, which is what full-duplex and turn-taking models need.\n- **Real rooms**: recorded on people's own devices in real environments, with unusable recordings filtered out by an ML screening pipeline.\n- **Metadata**: per-conversation fields such as topics, relationship, duration, turns per minute, turn-taking gap and speech dominance.\n\n## Why it matters\n\nFull-duplex voice agents trained only on polite, turn-by-turn speech fail when real users interrupt. Open Yap 1K positions itself as an alternative to Fisher, Switchboard and CANDOR for exactly that problem."
