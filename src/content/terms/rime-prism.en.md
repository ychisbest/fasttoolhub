explain: "## Key points\n\n1. **The problem it targets**: big-lab s2s models fuse text and audio at runtime, so you can't inspect or rewrite what the agent is about to say. Classic cascades (speech-to-text, LLM, text-to-speech) give control but throw away tone, hesitation, laughter and background sound.\n2. **How it works**: one end-to-end model that listens, writes what it will say, and keeps hearing the user while it speaks. The text is a **checkpoint**: the decision is made over audio, your rules read every word before it becomes speech, and the speech still carries the caller's emotion.\n3. **Extras at the checkpoint**: rewritable text for action and content tags, phonemic conditioning for names and account terms, and tool management for composable agent flows. Tool calls run in parallel with speech.\n4. **Speed**: about **300ms** response time per the launch post, and **0.57s** to first audio on the Artificial Analysis harness, about half the wait of GPT-Live-1, GPT-Realtime-2.1 and Gemini 3.8 Live.\n5. **Accuracy**: **56.6 on τ-Voice** (airline, retail and telecom customer-service calls) without a reasoning step, ahead of every non-reasoning realtime model from OpenAI and Google by 17 to 30 points. In telecom it scores **82.5 vs 45.6** for GPT-Live-1.\n6. **Ownership**: Rime says Prism is the first s2s model anyone can **fine-tune**, including with reinforcement learning on your own calls, and it can run on-premises, in your VPC or in the cloud. Dialpad is a design partner."
