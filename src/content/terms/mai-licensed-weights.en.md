intro: "**MAI Licensed Weights** is the weight-licensing program [Microsoft AI introduced on 17 November 2026](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-licensed-weights/) (Ignite · Models). It is meant to let organizations deploy and customize Microsoft AI models in their own Azure environments. The company’s argument is that more of what makes a business distinctive will live inside the models it runs, so customers want control over who can access those models, how they are adapted, what they cost to operate, and when they are upgraded.\n\nThis is not another public foundation-model name. It is a **delivery and customization offering** that sits alongside the Ignite model launches. The companion [Custom Models / microsoft-frontier-tuning](https://microsoft.ai/models/microsoft-frontier-tuning/) page covers the same program (“Tune your own”); only the official name MAI Licensed Weights is filed as a term.\n\nMicrosoft says customer data and adaptations remain the customer’s, and that it will use them to train its own models only with express agreement. Availability is not a self-serve public download: the post says MAI Licensed Weights “is coming soon through our Frontier Company engagements,” and the current path is to contact a Microsoft account team."
explain: "The announcement uses customer evaluations to argue that adapting MAI models on proprietary knowledge can reach frontier-level or better results on specialized tasks at a much lower cost. AT&T used MAI models to analyze 5G networks: Microsoft says they outperformed GPT-6.1 Sol on standard tasks at about 1/50th the cost, and reached frontier-level quality on more complex tasks at a significantly lower cost; a footnote says the customized models were MAI Code 2 Flash and Code 2. Woven by Toyota fine-tuned MAI Code 2 Flash on historical MISRA fixes and reported ready-to-merge fixes in 44% of cases where its existing frontier models still needed human rework, at less than one-tenth the cost. Alaska Airlines reports aircraft-maintenance report classification rising from 81.2% to 91.1% accuracy, beating the frontier models it compared. The footnotes stress that these are customer-specific evaluations whose results vary by workload, configuration, and methodology.\n\nCompared with the same day’s new model SKUs, Licensed Weights is about where a model runs, who can change it, and when it is upgraded, not about adding another public benchmark score. In the Ignite keynote, Satya Nadella also described it as giving trusted customers access to Microsoft AI model weights through Frontier Co."
usage: "According to the official getting-started note, MAI Licensed Weights is **coming soon** through Frontier Company engagements. To learn more, [contact a Microsoft account team](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-licensed-weights/) and see the companion [Custom Models page](https://microsoft.ai/models/microsoft-frontier-tuning/). There is no public self-serve download or general API toggle yet.\n\nThe intended use, as described, is to deploy selected MAI models in a customer’s own Azure environment, adapt them with proprietary knowledge and workflows, and keep control over deployment, access, and upgrade timing. The AT&T, Woven by Toyota, and Alaska Airlines examples are all customizations of concrete SKUs such as MAI Code 2 / Code 2 Flash, so outcomes depend on the base model, data, and evaluation task. Do not treat this as a new model ID you can pick today in OpenRouter or Copilot, and do not confuse it with the separately filed Code 2, Cyber 2 Flash, Realtime 1, or Image 2.7 entries."
