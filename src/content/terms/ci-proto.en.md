intro: "**ci-proto** is the first time-series foundation model (TSFM) family officially released by the London lab Continuous Intelligence on 12 November 2026. The official blog post [Introducing ci-proto](https://www.continuousintelligence.co.uk/news/ci-proto/), dated 12 November 2026, opens: “ci-proto is out today. It’s the debut model family from Continuous Intelligence and our first step towards a new generation of time series foundation models. It’s available to use now.”\n\nAt 12:15 UTC the same day, the company account [@Continu_Intel](https://x.com/Continu_Intel/status/2120852944056709139) posted that ci-proto is #1 on fev-bench, can recall patterns from more than 2 million observations ago, and is open-weight and available for commercial use. At 13:00 UTC, co-founder and CEO Benjamin Walker posted from [@benjaminwalker](https://x.com/benjaminwalker/status/2120864197340955028): “Today we're launching ci-proto, @Continu_Intel's first time-series foundation model,” listing the official claims of a fev-bench lead over Google, Amazon and Mistral, a context window 64 times that of other models, and open weights for commercial use. The lab was founded in London in July 2026 and is one of the teams selected for Stage 1 of SPRIND’s Next Frontier AI Challenge, with up to €3 million in funding.\n\nOfficially, ci-proto is a family of five zero-shot forecasting models: after pretraining on many datasets, it can forecast on unseen datasets without task-specific retraining. The largest member, ci-proto XL, has 167 million parameters and is still described as a prototype, but the official blog ranks it #1 among public foundation models on fev-bench (a multivariate forecasting benchmark for TSFMs) with a 51.5% skill score, ahead of TimeH2O-v1 (50.8%), Timestral-209M (50.4%), t0-gamma (49.4%), TimesFM-3 (49.0%) and Chronos-2 (47.3%). Those figures come from the official blog, with a cutoff of 11 November 2026, and the table excludes closed API models; this page does not independently reproduce them. The company also says the five sizes form the Pareto frontier for forecast error against model size and runtime: no other public TSFM, it claims, forecasts more accurately for the same size or speed.\n\nOn the method, the blog contrasts Transformer attention used by most existing TSFMs with ci-proto’s “attention-free time mixer”: attention grows quadratically with history length, while the time mixer grows only linearly, which the company says makes much longer context windows practical. Officially, ci-proto can use a context window 64 times as long as Mistral’s Timestral and can draw on patterns from more than 2 million observations ago. A scheduled power-outage example in the blog shows the forecast capturing a drop in electricity use only when an earlier outage is included in a four-year history. To run it, the company provides a Python package, `pip install continuousintelligence`, for local inference on CPU or GPU; users supply past observations and any known future covariates (such as operating schedules) and receive 9 forecast quantiles per target variable. Weights are on [Hugging Face: ContinuousIntelligence/ci-proto](https://huggingface.co/ContinuousIntelligence/ci-proto), and the code is at [github.com/continuousintelligence/ci](https://github.com/continuousintelligence/ci). The blog says the weights are available for commercial use. The company also has a newsletter and a waitlist for later models.\n\nA note on timing: the company account posted at 12:15 UTC (20:15 Asia/Shanghai), just before the previous 20:32 scan ended; the founder’s launch post was at 13:00 UTC (21:00 Asia/Shanghai), inside this slot.
