Its brief is to apply the company’s research to large, complex real-world systems, deepen the understanding of those systems, and deliver that work as value to Sakana AI’s clients. The official post is dated 24 November 2026 and lives at [sakana.ai/applied-research-lab](https://sakana.ai/applied-research-lab/). The official account [@SakanaAILabs](https://x.com/SakanaAILabs/status/2125046221631086970) introduced it at 01:57 UTC.\n\nThe lab is based at Sakana AI’s headquarters in Tokyo. Its first program is **Computational Finance and Economics (COFE)**; the company says other programs may follow. The announcement argues that a market or an economy is not something a single LLM-powered agent can resolve: it has to be modeled as a system of units, links, and knock-on effects. Agentic AI can now implement, test, and refine models at scale, but deciding which questions matter has remained expert work. The lab’s stated focus is combining the two.\n"
explain: "## What it is\n\nThis is not a new conversational model or a consumer app. It is a newly formed **applied research lab** inside Sakana AI. Officially, it takes research the company has pursued since its founding in July 2023—automated scientific discovery, evolutionary search, agent-based simulation, and evaluation—and applies it to complex real-world systems such as finance and the economy, including work for clients. The lab says it wants both a better understanding of system structure and the ability to scale and automate the building blocks of that understanding.\n\nCOFE is interested in the visible side of asset prices and in the real economy underneath them. In the company’s telling, finance has traditionally studied prices and economics the system beneath them; AI can connect the two at the granularity of firms and transactions, and in near real time, so that asset prices become a readable reflection of the economy.\n\n## The research it builds on\n\nThe announcement lists a set of earlier finance and economics projects as the lab’s most direct predecessors:\n\n- **EDINET-Bench** (ICLR 2026): evaluates LLMs on financial reasoning over ten years of Japanese corporate annual reports in a single-turn, non-agentic setting. A surprising result at the time was that even leading models were only marginally better than logistic regression on fraud detection and earnings forecasting.\n- **JFinBench**: evaluates LLMs on the 2026 editions of six Japanese financial certification exams, including the CPA and tax accountant exams.\n- **CoffeeBench** (NeurIPS 2026, with KPMG AZSA): tests whether LLM agents can run a business profitably for 90 days in a multi-agent coffee supply-chain economy.\n- **QD-Rec**: applies quality-diversity search to high-stakes decisions such as M&A, keeping an archive of diverse, well-reasoned candidates rather than a single ranked list.\n\nOn methods, the lab says it will focus in part on agent-based modeling. **Shachi** (ALIFE 2026) is a simulation framework for LLM-powered agents with modular minds (configuration, memory, and tools), used to study how collective behavior—such as a market’s reaction to a tariff shock—emerges. **CEDAR** (ALIFE 2026) uses LLM-guided tree search to discover and refine the structure of complex systems toward desired behaviors. Related company work includes **ShinkaEvolve** (ICLR 2026) and **AB-MCTS** (NeurIPS 2025) for search and for evolving code such as a trading strategy, and **The AI Scientist** (Nature, March 2026) for automating research from hypothesis to paper.\n"
