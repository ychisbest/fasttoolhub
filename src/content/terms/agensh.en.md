intro: "**Agensh** is a **decentralized multi-agent harness** open-sourced by **Microsoft Research** (MIT license). Its pitch: replace a central orchestrator with **self-organization**, and scale coding agents to a thousand concurrent workers.\\n\\nThe companion paper *Agensh: Scaling Organizational Intelligence to 1,024 Agents* (arXiv 2609.26781, 2026-09-22) is by Zhihao Zhan, Ting Song, Li Dong, Shaohan Huang, Jianxun Lian, Yan Xia, Furu Wei and coauthors. explain: "## How it works\\n\\nAgensh defines a repeatable **cooperation loop**: workers concurrently gather context → claim a sub-task → act → verify → merge progress asynchronously until the user goal is met. The loop sits on three light infrastructure pieces:\\n\\n- **Shared workspace** — experiments use **Gitea** for proposed / in-progress / completed work\\n- **Message interface** — **Mattermost** for worker-to-worker chat\\n- **Shared context** — adapted from DeLM, keeping reusable findings and work intentions\\n\\nIn the paper and README evaluations, Agensh is typically **stacked on each worker's own Copilot harness single-agent loop** — the product-facing Copilot harness stays the per-agent loop while Agensh adds team scale.\\n\\n## Main results (author-reported)\\n\\nOn the five hardest ProgramBench tasks (FFmpeg, gromacs, pandoc, PHP-src, ctags) with GPT-5.6-sol (high), a six-hour budget and no Internet, mean final test-pass rises from **19.31% with 1 agent** to **28.78% with 128 agents** (~49% relative). On **Pandoc**: **33.89% → 50.94% → 55.06%** at 1 → 128 → 1,024 agents. Larger teams also reach similar pass rates sooner. With GPT-6.1-sol (high), Pandoc six-hour pass rises from 56.19% (1 agent) to 79.59% (32 agents).\\n\\n> Figures are from the paper/README — not independently reproduced here; for reference only."
