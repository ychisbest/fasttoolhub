explain: "## The problem it targets\n\nThe official post and a16z’s note make a similar case: many robot skills look impressive in demos, but moving an object, changing the lighting, or introducing something unfamiliar can make them fail. Human demonstrations cannot cover every environment or every way a task can go wrong, and they do not produce enough diverse data on their own. Embo’s bet is that the next generation of frontier models should be **world models**—systems that learn how the physical world works, so a robot can anticipate the consequences of its actions, recover from mistakes, and adapt to situations it has not seen.\n\nThe company frames the work as two related problems: **generalization** (a skill learned in one kitchen should still work in a kitchen the robot has never entered) and **resilience** (if an object slips or a drawer sticks, the robot should recover and continue).\n\n## How it is approaching the problem\n\nEmbo says it is building **one unified multimodal model**, rather than bolting a language model onto robot control:\n\n- **Internet-scale pretraining:** The world model is pretrained from scratch on diverse videos and text, to learn about objects, physical interactions, and how tasks unfold. Predicting video is meant to fill gaps that individual demonstrations cannot cover.\n- **Fine-tuning on embodied data:** That knowledge is connected to real motors and sensors. The company says its humanoid fleet collects robot data and real-world feedback around the clock.\n- **Reinforcement learning in imagination:** The world model is also a practice ground. An agent rehearses motor skills across diverse imagined scenarios, including failures, before acting in the real world, with the aim of becoming more robust to unexpected situations.\n\nThe announcement argues that the information density of streaming video and high-frequency motor skills is something language models lack, so the work needs specialized architecture, algorithms, and infrastructure. The team previously built world-model agents and helped train multimodal models across about 70,000 GPUs. a16z also notes that Hafner’s Dreamer 4 learned to obtain diamonds in Minecraft from previously collected data, training its behavior entirely through imagined experience; Embo wants to take that style of learning to a much larger robotics scale. The company says it is focused on pushing general robot capabilities and will share progress as it unfolds.\n"
