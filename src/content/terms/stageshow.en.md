intro: "**Stageshow** is a free, open-source **agent skill** that teaches AI coding agents to produce polished **faux product UI** — marketing stills, cursor-driven demo animations, bento grids, phone mockups and dashboard scenes. It was released on **October 31, 2026** by **Benji Taylor**, head of design at X, and his launch post passed **4,700 likes** within hours, spreading quickly in English, Chinese, Japanese and Persian developer circles.\n\nTaylor's point is that most good models can already fake a UI, but the result usually looks *sloppy and heavy-handed*. Stageshow writes down the hundred small design decisions a designer makes by hand so an agent can make them too. It installs with one command — `npx skills add benjitaylor/skills --skill stageshow` — and works with any agent that supports skills, including Claude Code, Codex, Gemini and Grok Build. It is **MIT-licensed**."
explain: "## What's inside\n\n- **SKILL.md** — the core idea in one sentence (*\"It reads as a real product, simplified to what the viewer needs, framed with intent, and every motion has a cause\"*), the five common failure modes, a seven-step workflow and a table of gold examples.\n- **references/** — rules for craft (restraint, type, spacing, colour), motion (story, pace, the cursor), framing, grids, backgrounds, annotations, brand marks and filming, each with its reason and good/bad pairs.\n- **examples/** — dozens of gold examples the agent starts from: hero demos, bento grids, a phone swipe, a dashboard paired with a lock screen and more.\n- **kit/** — a small set of plain React parts (stage, windows, phones drawn from real geometry, frame-safe motion) that live in your codebase and can be restyled with your own design tokens.\n- **Scripts** — `film.mjs` films a demo and lays frames on a contact sheet, `jumps.mjs` catches one-frame snaps, and `layout.mjs` checks that text fits its boxes.\n\n## The five failure modes it targets\n\n1. **Too much** — real words in every cell, packed tight.\n2. **Too little** — a wireframe that doesn't read as a product.\n3. **Boxed** — the whole app shrunk into a card like a pasted screenshot.\n4. **Flat** — everything has the same visual weight.\n5. **Bland** — the brief's style never shows up.\n\nThe output is an ordinary React component, so the demo can be dropped into a landing page and edited like the rest of your code. Taylor notes most demos still take a few rounds of feedback, and that the skill may become unnecessary as model taste improves."
usage: "## How to use it\n\n1. Install: `npx skills add benjitaylor/skills --skill stageshow`.\n2. Ask your agent for something **small and specific**: one interaction, in one product, at one size — e.g. *\"A small looping demo for our homepage hero: someone assigns an issue to a teammate.\"*\n3. The agent reads the skill, starts from the closest gold example, builds the demo, **films it** and fixes what it finds.\n4.
