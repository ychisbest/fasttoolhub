explain: "Officially, Neon sits in the middle of the Gemini 4 family: Carbon is for the hardest, longest-horizon projects, Hydrogen is for fast everyday execution, and Neon is meant to balance depth and efficiency on complex, high-frequency workflows. Pricing on the blog is $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached input tokens (a 95% discount, Google’s figure), and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window. At its default high thinking level, Google says Neon pairs a high Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score with 32% lower cost per task than Carbon. The xhigh thinking level is where it posts its highest intelligence score, still described as 36%+ cheaper per task than competing models at their highest setting. These are company claims, not independent reruns by this radar.\n\nFigures stated in the official blog include: a score of 61 and #3 overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh; #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%), described as being on the Pareto frontier; #2 on steerability and a global score of 1336 on the new Agent Arena Index (4,200+ real-world agent sessions). For coding, Google says Neon ranks #1 overall on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index (58.6), scores 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and 46.2% at default high on FrontierCode 2.0, and on SWE-sweep leads the mini-SWE harness at 12.8% and reaches 39.0% in Google Antigravity. For science and math, the blog lists Terminal-Bench Science 70.5%, RiemannBench 84.0%, and HLE-Diamond 71.3%. The model card also tabulates some of these results next to Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol, and lists CursorBench 4.1 at 65.4% (58.1% for Claude Opus 5.5 in that table). Cite the 18 November 2026 blog or model card when repeating any of these numbers.\n\nIn Google Antigravity, the blog says Neon can run long, tool-heavy work on its own or act as a fast, high-judgment Orchestrator directing parallel Hydrogen subagents. It also introduces “Gemini 4 Neon for cyber,” aimed at secure code generation, penetration testing, and vulnerability finding, validation, and patching, rolling out in the coming days to trusted defenders in the Fairwind Program. On safety, Google says Neon builds on the Gemini 4 safety and security stack and keeps Carbon-level protections against severe misuse and prompt injection; the full Safety & Security Report is described as coming in the following days.\n\nAvailability follows the blog’s “Start building” section: developers can use the Gemini API paid tier in Google AI Studio and Android Studio, and paid Pro or Ultra subscribers can try Neon in Antigravity and AI Studio; enterprises can use it in Gemini for business (API or model dropdown) and Antigravity; consumers who are Google AI Pro or Ultra subscribers can select it in the Gemini app; cyber access is requested through the Cloud account team for the Fairwind Program. Inputs include text, images, audio, and video. The model card describes a combined input-output context of up to 1M tokens, with up to 64K default synchronous output tokens and up to 1M via background long decode."
