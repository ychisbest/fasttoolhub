explain: "## What the authors claim\n\nThe project asks whether a general-purpose language model can be taught to fold proteins, and what learning to fold enables beyond structure prediction. RumiFold is a pretrained chat model that predicts structures directly from its own hidden states through repeated folding passes, then answers questions in natural language from those folded representations.\n\nThe authors say folding accuracy is comparable to dedicated single-representation models such as **SimpleFold**, with state-of-the-art results on Gene Ontology protein-function prediction and free-text function description. They also argue that folding organizes the model’s hidden states around protein function, creating an internal per-residue workspace that the model uses when it answers. The paper reports causal interventions in support of the claim that this folded workspace links structure and language.\n\n## Architecture (from the project page)\n\nRumiFold is built on **Qwen3-8B** and fine-tuned with LoRA adapters. It has no pair representation, no multiple sequence alignment, and no diffusion module. Computation happens in three steps:\n\n1. **Embed.** A frozen **ESM-C** encoder reads the amino-acid sequence. A small trained bridge turns those embeddings into protein tokens the language model can read, one per residue.\n2. **Fold.** The language model processes the protein tokens in repeated passes with the same weights. A single linear head reads the backbone structure directly out of the hidden states.\n3. **Answer.** The same model then reads the question alongside its own folded hidden states and replies in natural language.\n\nAgainst single-sequence folding models, the page says RumiFold matches SimpleFold on **FoldBench** and **CASP16**. An example FoldBench TM-score row lists ESMFold at **0.871**, RumiFold at **0.840**, and SimpleFold 3B at **0.840**. ESMFold, which uses a pair representation, remains ahead, and models given an MSA are stronger still. On the function side, the authors highlight GO annotation and free-text descriptions, and they frame the work as a step toward “native biomolecular intelligence,” where protein-based computation and language-based reasoning are integrated capabilities of a general-purpose language model.\n"
