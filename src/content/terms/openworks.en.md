The official account [@OpenAI](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2124922133545714160) introduced it at 17:44 UTC, [@gdb](https://x.com/gdb/status/2124940416072200521) followed with “Introducing OpenWorks,” and the official page [openai.com/openworks](https://openai.com/openworks/) first appeared in this scan’s sitemap diff.\n\nOpenAI’s pitch is that partners spend days each month on projects the owner chooses, host a monthly community workshop, and leave the team able to keep using the tools on their own. explain: "## How the program works\n\nThe official page splits OpenWorks into two sides.\n\n**Become an OpenWorks Partner.** OpenAI is recruiting its first partners: technically skilled individuals and small teams willing to help local businesses, including software and AI consultancies applying alongside existing client work. Partners dedicate days each month to in-person work and host a monthly workshop. In return, OpenAI offers a partner community, tools and workshop support, and a stipend of up to **$8,000** per month.\n\n**Work with a local partner.** Independently owned U.S. small businesses—restaurants, retailers, service firms, manufacturers, and the like—can apply at no cost and with no technical background required. Together they pick a task that matters, put AI to work, and build skills the team can use independently. Businesses also get **three months of ChatGPT Business** at no cost. Software and SaaS companies, venture-backed businesses, and private-equity-owned businesses are not eligible for the pilot.\n\nThe pilot map covers the Midwest, South, West, and Northeast, including Chicago, Kansas City, Fargo, Madison, Cleveland, South Bend, St. Louis, Jackson, Charlotte, Oklahoma City, Charleston, Nashville, Austin, Houston, Atlanta, Los Angeles, Incline Village, Olympia, Seattle, and New York.\n\n## Official case studies\n\nThe page highlights local examples: Tiny Things, a miniatures shop in Fargo, built a ChatGPT inventory dashboard that the owner estimates would have cost about $50,000 to commission; Ghost Grow in Charleston used ChatGPT for restaurant outreach and says its response rate rose from 20% to 75%; Sign Badgers in Fargo used Codex and ChatGPT to track inventory and vendor prices, cutting checks that once took hours down to about 15–20 minutes. These are organizer case studies; results will vary.\n"
