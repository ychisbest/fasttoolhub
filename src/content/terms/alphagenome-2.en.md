intro: "**AlphaGenome 2** is the next-generation DNA sequence model Google DeepMind announced on 11 November 2026. The official write-up is [AlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand disease](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/), by Adam Gayoso, Natasha Latysheva, Pushmeet Kohli and Žiga Avsec. The official account [@GoogleDeepMind](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2120532824465805749) posted at 2026-11-11T15:03:17Z, calling it “our new AI model that zooms in on specific cells to help scientists understand what drives disease.”\n\nThe blog opens with a basic fact of biology: every cell in the body carries the same DNA blueprint, yet each cell type and state — a heart muscle cell, or an immune cell fighting an infection — switches different genes on or off to do its job. Global efforts such as the Human Cell Atlas are mapping this gene expression for millions of cells, but predicting how subtle DNA variations affect that expression in specific cells remains an unsolved challenge, and a key driver of human traits and disease. Last year DeepMind launched AlphaGenome to predict how DNA variants affect molecular activity across broad tissues, then expanded its reach with AlphaGenome Atlas, a platform of predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome. AlphaGenome 2 zooms in from those broad tissue averages to individual cell types and cell states, and redesigned the architecture. The company says this is a step-change in variant predictive performance, and that the model can also make predictions for cell and disease states it has not seen before.\n\nOn training and capabilities, the official blog says the model was trained on both bulk and single-cell data from human and mouse samples, including over 107 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues. Instead of predicting many tissues in parallel, AlphaGenome 2 is a conditional model: it predicts DNA effects for one condition — a tissue, cell type or state — at a time, and uses that target biological condition as input at every stage of its architecture, so it can learn how the same stretch of DNA is read differently across cell types. Besides the core readouts of the original model — gene expression, histone modifications and chromatin accessibility — it also predicts DNA methylation. A conditional design also allows cross-modality imputation: if a scientist inputs just one measurement, such as single-cell gene activity, the model can predict unmeasured properties such as chromatin accessibility. Scientists can also upload a single-cell RNA-seq snapshot from just a few dozen cells; that activity is compressed into a cell-state representation, and the model can then be conditioned on unseen states without retraining. Official examples include: on the TenK10K map of 28 primary immune cell types, AlphaGenome 2 reliably identified nearly twice as many causal variants (30% vs. 16%) and directional gene effects (43% vs. 23%) as the original AlphaGenome fine-tuned on single-cell data and other specialised tools; it also computationally reproduced a landmark finding that a variant in a distant regulatory switch drives IBD by overactivating ETS2 in gut macrophages, leaving other cell types and genes largely unaffected. Given unseen single-cell data from a COVID-19 mRNA vaccine study, it distinguished vaccine-activated immune T cells from resting ones and showed that a variant linked to severe COVID-19 disrupts gene activity more strongly in the activated state.\n\nOn availability, the blog says AlphaGenome 2 and interactive Colab tutorials for expression conditioning are available today for non-commercial research via the AlphaGenome API, and in private preview for commercial research. DeepMind also says it will soon release AlphaGenome 2 Skills in Google Antigravity. The post is explicit: AlphaGenome and AlphaGenome 2 are intended for research use only and have not been validated or approved for clinical or diagnostic purposes.\n\nKey links:\n\n- Official blog: https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/ ;\n- Official post: https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2120532824465805749 (lead post at 2026-11-11T15:03:17Z, follow-ups at 15:03:18Z and 15:03:19Z);\n- This is a **specific model name from Google DeepMind**, the next generation after the 2025 AlphaGenome.
