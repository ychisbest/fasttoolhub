intro: "**Aure** is the Voice AI translation model DeepL unveiled on 11 November 2026. Officially, it is DeepL’s most natural and lifelike voice translation model yet, and it now powers DeepL Voice. The product page is [Introducing Aure](https://www.deepl.com/products/voice/aure). The press release is on [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deepl-unveils-aure-its-most-lifelike-voice-ai-translation-model-yet-302935694.html) and is listed on DeepL’s [press page](https://www.deepl.com/en/press-release). The official account [@DeepLcom](https://x.com/DeepLcom/status/2120430075149443191) posted at 2026-11-11T08:15:00Z. In the release, founder and CEO Jarek Kutylowski said Aure is meant to carry even subtle shifts in expression into another language, in the speaker’s own voice and in real time.\n\nOfficially, DeepL had already launched voice preservation, so a cross-language conversation could keep a recognizable personal voice instead of a generic synthetic one. Aure is the next step: it is not only “whose voice this is,” but “how they are speaking right now.” Previously, DeepL Voice largely decided the translated voice from a static sample at the start of a conversation, which could not follow a change in emotion, emphasis or pace, or delivery that contradicts the words, such as sarcasm. Aure uses both the translated words and the source audio, and starts adapting before a sentence is finished. DeepL says that means isolating the speaker’s voice identity and prosody in noisy settings, filtering background noise and echo, and carrying a voice across languages without exaggerating an accent or distorting how someone sounds. The company also says it does not permanently store meeting transcripts or translations, and deletes this data from memory when a session ends, so Aure has to adapt to each speaker in real time without a long-term cross-language sample.\n\nOn the method, DeepL says it combined training techniques that separate voice identity, prosody and spoken content, then used reinforcement learning so translation accuracy and speech quality stay intact while the translated voice sounds natural and free of accent. The product page says Aure matches a voice dynamically during the live session, without creating or storing a permanent voice profile; data is encrypted in transit and is not used to train DeepL’s models. DeepL Voice also filters background noise, other speakers and echoes so it can work in open offices or on a factory floor. The page states that, in online meetings, DeepL Voice has 76% fewer critical translation errors than platform-native translation tools; that figure is the company’s, and we have not independently reproduced it.\n\nNo extra setup is required. Aure is applied automatically across DeepL Voice for Online Meetings (Microsoft Teams, Zoom and Google Meet), DeepL Voice for In-Person Conversations, and the DeepL API for Voice. It initially supports eight languages — English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese and Korean — with more to follow. For languages Aure does not yet cover, DeepL Voice automatically uses the previous voice models. The release names Pioneer Corporation, Aramark and Avendra International, and Inetum as organizations already using DeepL Voice, and notes that DeepL Voice was listed in Fast Company’s 2026 Next Big Things in Tech awards in the Applied AI category.\n\nKey links:\n\n- Product: https://www.deepl.com/products/voice/aure ; press release: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deepl-unveils-aure-its-most-lifelike-voice-ai-translation-model-yet-302935694.html ;\n- Official post: https://x.com/DeepLcom/status/2120430075149443191 ; DeepL Voice: https://www.deepl.com/en/products/voice ;\n- This is a **specific DeepL speech-translation model name**.
