intro: "**SolveIt** (also styled Solveit on the official site) is the name of the learning and R&D workspace Answer.AI opened to the public on 16–17 November 2026, after using it internally. Jeremy Howard ([@jeremyphoward](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2122487017137398256)) posted at 2026-11-17T00:28:33Z (08:28:33 Beijing time on 17 November 2026): “We built a tool for our own internal team at AnswerAI to let us learn, explore, & develop mastery with the help of AI: SolveIt. Today, we’re releasing it to the public for the 1st time.” At scan time the post had about 642 likes and about 33,000 impressions.\n\nThe official [fast.ai post](https://www.fast.ai/posts/2026-11-16-solveit-launch/) is dated 16 November 2026, titled “Announcing the Public Launch of SolveIt,” and credited to Jeremy Howard and Rachel Thomas. It says Answer.AI built the tool so people can learn, explore, and develop mastery with AI’s help, rather than automating humans out of the process. Co-founder Eric Ries (author of *The Lean Startup* and *Incorruptible*) is quoted as saying there is a “moral imperative” to share it more widely. Joining costs nothing and you pay only for what you use, at [solve.it.com](https://solve.it.com). Over the past two years they invited about 3,000 preview users and share a busy Discord server with them.\n\nThe post describes SolveIt as a shared workspace where humans, AI, code, notes, documents, and outputs live and work together, used for software development, research experiments, teaching, and learning. It contrasts this with Claude or ChatGPT, where you type into a box and the message is appended at the bottom of a conversation: in SolveIt, your documents, code, and the AI share a dedicated server, and the AI runs code in the same interpreter you use. Work happens in an editable dialog where code, notes, heading hierarchies, exploration, and AI discussion are interspersed. You can edit, move, or delete material, and insert a prompt anywhere; the AI only sees what comes before that point, so you control what it sees. Each SolveIt instance is a persistent server with a terminal, a file system, and git; you can clone a repo, explore it with the AI, and push changes, and you can create as many instances as you like. The authors also write that they have started writing SolveIt in SolveIt. They suggest starting with the first lesson of the “How to Solve It with Code” course, and point to Jeremy’s AI Engineer Conference keynote *Growing on Purpose*.\n\nThe official site [solve.it.com](https://solve.it.com) is tagged “Take back your agency.” It says “Solveit is here to help you achieve mastery and love your craft,” and “Solveit is built to fight cognitive offloading.” Examples on the site include close-reading and replicating a paper in the same dialog, and turning what you learn into a live web app (demo hosts use solveit.pub). The site also says the Solveit method is about working in small, iterative steps that you drive, and that it can be used for reading papers, studying mathematics, reviewing contracts, analyzing data, writing books, and developing software.\n\nKey points:\n\n- Official announcement: https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2122487017137398256 ; official blog: https://www.fast.ai/posts/2026-11-16-solveit-launch/ ; product site: https://solve.it.com ;\n- This is **a concrete product name** for the workspace Answer.AI has now opened to the public. Do not describe it as a generic ChatGPT or Claude chat-box substitute, or as a still-private preview codename. About 3,000 people were in the preview; public-release scope and pricing are as stated by Answer.AI, and this page does not guarantee them."
